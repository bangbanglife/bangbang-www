Operator: BangBang Life Pty Ltd (ACN 703 821 738), referred to below as “the Platform” or “we”.

Where it applies: Australia.

Languages: these Rules are issued in Chinese and in English. Both versions have equal effect. If the two versions differ in meaning, the English version prevails.

1. Scope and basic principles

1.1 These Rules explain how payments, release, bank payouts, refunds and disputes work on the BangBang app. They form part of the Terms of Service and apply to paid tasks paid for through the Platform. The “Task Poster” is the user who posts a task and pays for it, and the “Service Provider” is the user who takes the task on and provides the service. “Starting the service” means the Service Provider tapping “Start Task” in the app.

1.2 The Platform is not a party to the task contract. The third-party payment provider Stripe processes payments. Under clause 2.3 of the Terms of Service, the Platform acts as the Service Provider's limited payment collection agent. Once the task amount is successfully charged, the Task Poster is treated as having paid that amount to the Service Provider. The task amount, less the platform service fee described in section 8, goes into the Service Provider's Stripe payment account. The Platform does not keep users' task funds in its own bank account.

1.3 Release means that the funds may enter the process for payout to the Service Provider's bank account. Bank payout means that the payment provider actually initiates a transfer to that account. The only ordinary outcomes within the Platform are full release and full refund. The Platform does not split the amount and does not offer partial refunds. Any remedy required by law is unaffected.

1.4 The Platform's dispute decision is final only for handling within the Platform. It is not a judgment or an arbitration, and it does not determine the parties' rights at law. You may still use the external options in clause 6.6.

1.5 These Rules do not exclude, restrict or modify any consumer guarantee, right or remedy you have under the Australian Consumer Law that cannot lawfully be excluded.

1.6 “Business day” is counted under clause 1.5 of the Terms of Service, with one calendar for users throughout Australia. The Platform's handling targets and reply periods (sections 7 and 9) are counted in business days. Periods counted in hours or days, such as 24 hours, 48 hours, 7 days and 30 days, run continuously and are not affected by weekends or public holidays. Banks and card issuers count their own business days, which this definition does not cover (see clause 4.1).

2. Payment

2.1 The Task Poster must complete payment within 30 minutes of selecting a Service Provider, whether by selecting an offer or by accepting an application. When payment succeeds, the amount is charged and the task contract is formed (see clause 5.2 of the Terms of Service). A task with an agreed start time and an “as soon as possible” task use the same payment window. If there is no valid payment by the end of the window, the selection expires and clause 5.3 applies.

2.2 The task amount range is shown in the app and is currently A$5 to A$1,000. Until a new Task Poster's first task has been completed and released, a lower limit on the amount of each task may apply, as shown in the app. Changes to the range do not affect existing task contracts. The app shows the supported payment methods, which currently include cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay. Card payments may require verification by the card issuer (3D Secure). The card statement normally shows BANGBANG followed by the last few digits of the task number.

2.3 The Platform marks a task as confirmed and paid only after it receives confirmation of a valid payment. If the outcome of a payment is unclear, the Platform checks it first and does not charge again. Conflicting operations are not carried out on the same payment at the same time, and a payment is not refunded or released twice.

3. Release and bank payout

3.1 Release on confirmation: when the Task Poster confirms that the task is complete and none of the matters in clause 3.2 prevents release, the Platform releases the funds. The Platform may also release funds under a dispute decision under section 6.

3.1A Submitting completion: when the Service Provider submits completion, they must attach at least one photo. A screenshot may be used for an online task. For other tasks (at an address, pickup and delivery, or in a specified area), the Service Provider must also grant location permission. The Platform takes one reading of the device's current location as evidence of completion (see clause 4.6 of the Privacy Policy). If permission is granted but a location cannot be obtained at the time, completion can still be submitted and the record will say “location not obtained”.

3.2 Automatic confirmation and release: if 48 hours have passed since the Service Provider submitted completion (this moment is the “automatic confirmation time”), the Task Poster has not confirmed completion, neither party has raised a dispute under clause 6.1, and none of the following applies, the Platform automatically confirms completion and releases the funds:

· an unresolved dispute, a refund request in progress or an open no-show report;

· a chargeback on the payment, or a check by the Platform of the payment's status;

· a safety or fraud review of the task that the Platform has not yet finished;

· either party's account is banned or is no longer in normal use;

· the Platform is handling the funds for the task, for example after an account ban (see clause 7.9).

In those cases the Platform does not confirm automatically. The 30-day restrictions in clauses 7.6 and 7.8 are not among them and do not stop automatic confirmation and release. The time limit in clause 4.5 applies separately and takes priority over this clause.

3.3 Notice and reminders: the Platform tells the Task Poster the automatic confirmation time by push notification, and also by SMS if SMS notifications are on. If 24 hours after submission nothing has been done and the automatic confirmation time has not yet arrived, it sends one more reminder. A Task Poster who is not satisfied with the service should raise a dispute under clause 6.1 before that time. Automatic release does not mean the Task Poster accepted the quality of the service, and it does not affect their rights under law.

3.4 Delaying release and bank payout: under clause 2.3 of the Terms of Service, bank payout is scheduled only after the funds are released. If a dispute, an open no-show report or a chargeback on a payment is unresolved, the Platform delays release and bank payout only for the amount in question, and for no longer than the time limit in clause 4.5. The Platform tells both parties the reason and how the matter will be handled, and publishes the decision rules in sections 6 and 8. Independent restrictions imposed by a payment provider or card issuer, or under law, follow their own rules.

3.5 After release: the Platform's ordinary refund process ends when the funds are released. After release, a concern about performance may be taken up with the other party or pursued through the options in clause 6.6. Any refund or other remedy required by law is unaffected. Clause 3.6 on correcting errors and clause 8.5 on chargebacks still apply.

3.6 Technical or operational errors: where the Platform finds a technical or operational error by the Platform or the payment provider, such as a payment processed twice, an incorrect amount or a payment to the wrong account, it corrects only the error itself, for example by returning an overcharge to the payer or paying a shortfall. It does not use the correction to change a release or refund decision already made. The Platform notifies the affected parties and explains why. If you disagree, you can contact support@bangbanglife.com.au. A concern about performance itself is not covered by this clause.

3.7 Bank payout: each day, the Platform schedules bank payouts for funds that have been released and are available for payout. Actual receipt depends on Stripe and the receiving bank. If the payment account is restricted or a bank payout fails, the Platform tells the Service Provider to check their payout details or contact support. The Platform does not offer instant payouts.

4. Refunds

4.1 Full refund to the original payment method: before release, a refund approved or made automatically under these Rules returns the full amount actually charged to the original payment method. It is not converted into a Platform balance, points or vouchers. A BB Voucher that was used is returned separately under the applicable voucher rules, and this does not replace the refund of the amount paid. Refunds generally arrive in around 5 to 10 bank business days. Banks and card issuers count their own business days, which differ from the Platform's; actual timing depends on the payment provider and the card issuer. After a full refund, the task ends.

4.2 Cancellation before work starts: after the charge and before the service starts, if the Task Poster or the Service Provider cancels under clause 5.1, the Platform automatically makes a full refund. If a no-show report, dispute or chargeback on the task is unresolved, the Platform first handles it under the relevant rules and does not refund twice.

4.3 No-show: after payment is confirmed and before the Service Provider submits completion, either party may report the other as a no-show in the app and attach photos or an explanation. A further case of this kind is not opened while a dispute, refund request or no-show report is already in progress. The funds are not released automatically while a report is open; for what happens when the time limit in clause 4.5 is reached, see that clause.

· Service Provider no-show: if the Platform verifies it, it makes a full refund under clause 4.1. When verifying, the Platform may ask the Service Provider for their account. A Service Provider who disagrees with the finding or the action taken can contact support or appeal under clause 7.5.

· Task Poster no-show: it can be reported after the agreed start time, or, for an “as soon as possible” task, from the time payment is confirmed. The Platform notifies the Task Poster and gives them 24 hours to respond. The outcomes are as follows.

· If during the response period the Task Poster does not respond or accepts the no-show: the Platform makes a full refund, closes the task, takes action under clause 7.6 and tells the Service Provider. If the payment has already been refunded, no further refund is made. Any earlier refund request closes at the same time.

· If during the response period the Task Poster denies the no-show: the report becomes a dispute under section 6. If the report cannot be converted in the app because of a system problem, the Platform records the response and handles the matter manually, and does not penalise the Task Poster because of that. After the response period ends, the Task Poster can no longer respond to the report by raising a dispute; if they disagree with the outcome they can appeal under clause 7.5.

· If during the response period the Service Provider submits completion: the Service Provider's report of the Task Poster's no-show closes automatically, without action against the Task Poster. If the Task Poster has already requested a refund, the Service Provider may report the Task Poster's no-show through support. The Platform pauses the refund request and gives the Task Poster the same 24 hours to respond. If the Task Poster disputes the report, the two cases are handled together as a dispute.

· If no payment was made or the report is not upheld: the Platform takes no action on the funds because of the report.

4.4 Refund requests: only the Task Poster may request a refund. All of the following must apply: payment has been confirmed, the Service Provider has not submitted completion, the funds have not been released, the Platform is not checking the payment's status, and there is no unresolved dispute. The request is for a full refund. Only one request may be open per task at a time, and the Task Poster may withdraw it. After the Service Provider submits completion and before release, a Task Poster who disagrees with the performance raises a dispute under clause 6.1. A refund request that becomes a dispute under these Rules is handled under section 6.

Before approving a refund, the Platform notifies the Service Provider and gives them up to 24 hours to respond. The Platform does not approve the refund during that period unless the Service Provider has already responded or there is an urgent personal safety matter; a rejection does not have to wait for the response period. At the refund request stage, the Service Provider's response and attachments are for the Platform's reference only. Once the request becomes a dispute, both parties may submit material in the dispute and both have the chance to respond to the other's material. The Platform tells the parties the reason for approving or rejecting the request and the options that remain. Once a refund is approved, it is made under clause 4.1.

4.5 85 days from payment: this is the longest the Platform will keep funds that have not been released, because the payment provider limits how long money can be kept before it is paid out. The time is counted from the moment the charge succeeds, in consecutive days, including weekends and public holidays. If the funds have still not been released on day 85:

· where there is no unresolved dispute, chargeback or open no-show report, the Platform automatically cancels the task and refunds the full amount to the Task Poster's original payment method;

· where there is an unresolved dispute or open no-show report, the Platform likewise refunds the full amount to the Task Poster's original payment method, and the dispute or report is closed. This is not a decision on who was right. It does not find either party to be in breach or a no-show, and it does not count towards clause 7.6 or 7.8. A Service Provider who considers the money is owed to them may still seek to recover it from the Task Poster through the civil tribunal in their own state or territory, or through a court with jurisdiction;

· where there is a chargeback, clause 8.5 applies.

If a refund or other money operation on the payment is already under way, the payment is not refunded twice. Automatic cancellation and the refund above do not affect either party's rights under law.

5. Cancellation, payment window and rescheduling

5.1 Cancellation: the Task Poster may close the task before selecting a Service Provider, and Service Providers who have made offers are notified. Cancellation after selecting a Service Provider and before payment does not involve funds. After the charge and before the service starts, the Task Poster or the Service Provider may cancel the task and the Platform automatically makes a full refund (see clause 4.2). Once the service has started, neither party can end the task unilaterally through ordinary cancellation. Before completion is submitted, the Task Poster may request a refund under clause 4.4 and either party may report the other as a no-show under clause 4.3. After completion is submitted, a dispute is raised under clause 6.1. An open no-show report, dispute or chargeback must be dealt with first.

5.2 The Platform records cancellations, no-shows and payment timeouts. Repeated occurrences may be dealt with under section 7. A verified Service Provider no-show is dealt with under clause 7.8.

5.3 Payment window: the payment window starts under clause 2.1 when the Task Poster selects a Service Provider, and the deadline shown in the app applies. The Platform reminds the Task Poster before the window closes. If the Task Poster has not paid in time, the selection expires, the task reopens and the selected Service Provider is notified. An unclear payment outcome is handled under clause 2.3. Where the Service Provider was selected by selecting an offer, once the same offer has returned to available status twice because of the Task Poster's payment timeout, the third timeout invalidates that offer. The following are not counted: where the timeout is caused by the Platform pausing payments, a restriction on the Task Poster's account or a declined payment, the offer returns to available status; where the selected Service Provider's account is restricted and cannot receive payment, or one party has blocked the other, the offer expires.

5.4 Rescheduling: for a task with an agreed start time, after a Service Provider has been selected and before the service starts, the parties may reschedule by agreement, once per task. An “as soon as possible” task has no agreed start time, and changes may be discussed in chat. If the date must be changed, cancel and repost. Rescheduling changes only the time, not the amount or scope; to change the amount or scope, cancel and repost. Rescheduling does not change the time limit in clause 4.5, which still runs from the time the charge succeeds.

6. Disputes

6.1 Raising a dispute: after the Service Provider submits completion in the app, either party may raise a dispute directly in the app until the Task Poster confirms completion or the automatic confirmation time arrives, whichever comes first. Once the automatic confirmation time arrives, a dispute can no longer be raised and the funds are then released, except that where automatic confirmation does not take place because of a matter listed in clause 3.2, a dispute can still be raised until release. For matters before completion is submitted, see clause 4.3 on no-show reports and clause 4.4 on refund requests; this timing restriction does not apply to a no-show report or refund request that becomes a dispute under these Rules. Only one dispute may be open per task at a time. There is no automatic release while a dispute is unresolved, and the funds are handled under clause 3.4.

6.2 Evidence: both parties may provide explanations, photographs and other evidence. The Platform may retrieve chat records relating to the task, gives both parties a reasonable opportunity to give their account and respond, and protects personal information as required by law. The Platform keeps a complete case file. A party may download a copy of the dispute record (party copy), which excludes reporter identities, internal Platform records and internal identifiers. The complete file is used for the Platform's handling or provided as required by law.

6.3 Progress: the Platform keeps both parties informed of the progress of the case and aims to make its decision before the time limit in clause 4.5 runs out. If there is still no decision by then, clause 4.5 applies.

6.4 Platform decision: if both parties agree, the Platform carries out the full release or full refund that these Rules permit. If they do not agree, the Platform reviews both parties' materials and the relevant task records, acts reasonably, and makes a final decision, for the purposes of the Platform's own process, on full release or full refund. It tells both parties the outcome, the main reasons and the external options. The Platform charges its platform service fee only when funds are released, and the fee is returned together with a full refund; the Platform does not favour either party because of this when it decides.

6.5 Published decision rules: the Platform publishes the applicable rules for release, refunds and disputes. In deciding, it considers the agreed scope and time of the task, the records of the start and completion of the service, the facts of any no-show or cancellation, both parties' accounts and the material they submit, and the applicable payment, refund and no-show rules. This clause applies equally when the Platform deals with refund requests and no-show reports. The Platform assesses the material by asking which party's account is more likely to be true. If one party submits no material, the Platform decides on what it has. Applicable statutory rights and remedies take priority.

6.6 External options: the Platform's decision ends only its own dispute process. You may still ask the consumer protection agency in your state or territory, or the civil tribunal there, for help or resolution (for example Consumer Affairs Victoria and VCAT in Victoria, or NSW Fair Trading and NCAT in New South Wales), or seek resolution through a court with jurisdiction, subject to the applicable procedures. Where the two parties to an online task are in different states or territories, clause 1.6 of the Terms of Service applies. You need not give up any right or remedy you have by law.

6.7 Abuse: where the evidence establishes that a dispute was used in bad faith to obtain an improper benefit, the Platform may take action on the account under section 7. Action on an account does not itself change what happens to the funds.

6.8 Restrictions while a case is open: while a dispute, refund request or no-show report is open, neither party can post new tasks or submit new offers or applications until the case is closed. Selecting offers and paying for the parties' other existing tasks continue as normal.

6.9 Further materials: where the Platform asks for further materials, the time allowed is fixed at 48 hours from the time the Platform makes the request. This clause applies to refund requests and to a Task Poster's report of a Service Provider no-show. It does not apply to disputes: a dispute is not rejected for missing materials, and the Platform decides on the materials available under clause 6.4. If the time passes without the materials, the refund request is rejected automatically or the no-show report is closed automatically, and the Platform tells the applicant why. After that, as long as the Service Provider has not yet submitted completion and the request or report still meets the conditions in clause 4.4 or 4.3, the applicant may add the missing materials and submit it again, as many times as needed.

7. Account governance and appeals

7.1 No automatic bans: a decision to ban an account is made by Platform staff after a manual review of the evidence, which may include dispute records, reports and audit records.

7.2 Warning before a ban: where a ban is being considered because of conduct or performance, the Platform first gives a written warning stating the reasons and allows 7 days to respond. Where personal safety, fraud, a law-enforcement requirement or serious misconduct is involved, the Platform may ban the account immediately.

7.3 A ban is a full ban: once it takes effect, the account cannot sign in and any existing sessions end immediately.

7.4 Notice: warnings, restrictions and bans, and the revocation or lifting of any of them, are notified to you by SMS, which does not depend on signing in to the app. If you have a linked and verified email address, an email is sent as well. The notice states the reasons and how to appeal, and says that you may appeal within 30 days and that the Platform will reply within 10 business days.

7.5 Appeals: you may appeal within 30 days of the date the action is taken (you are notified at the same time), by writing to support@bangbanglife.com.au with your explanation and evidence. If your account has not been banned, you may also appeal in the app. The Platform replies within 10 business days and either upholds or revokes the action. Actions and appeals are recorded.

7.6 Graduated action for Task Poster no-shows (see clause 4.3): this is based on the number of confirmed and unrevoked Task Poster no-shows in the previous 6 months. First occurrence: a written warning. Second occurrence: a 30-day restriction on posting or editing tasks, selecting offers and paying. Third and later occurrences: the same 30-day restriction, and Platform staff review whether to ban the account (any ban follows clauses 7.1 and 7.2). During a restriction you can still close a task for which no offer has been selected, and tasks already paid for continue as normal. Every action is notified to the Task Poster and may be appealed under clause 7.5; if an appeal is upheld, the action is revoked and that no-show no longer counts. The Service Provider is told only the outcome, with no other information about the Task Poster.

7.7 A ban does not take away any contractual right or legal remedy you already have.

7.8 Graduated action for Service Provider no-shows (see clause 4.3), which mirrors clause 7.6: this is based on the number of confirmed and unrevoked Service Provider no-shows in the previous 6 months. First occurrence: a written warning. Second occurrence: a 30-day restriction on taking on tasks, during which you cannot submit or change offers or applications. Third and later occurrences: the same 30-day restriction, and Platform staff review whether to ban the account (any ban follows clauses 7.1 and 7.2). Tasks already confirmed are not affected. Every action is notified to the Service Provider and may be appealed under clause 7.5; if an appeal is upheld, the action is revoked and that no-show no longer counts.

7.9 Paid tasks when an account is banned: when either party is banned and the service has not started, Platform staff usually cancel the task within 1 business day and make a refund under clause 4.1; if a dispute or chargeback is unresolved, sections 6 and 8 apply. If the service has started or completion has been submitted, automatic confirmation stops, Platform staff contact both parties and handle the matter under section 6, and the ban alone does not change where the funds go. For safety, compliance, fraud-risk or account-governance reasons, the Platform may cancel a task that has not begun and whose funds have not been released, make a refund under clause 4.1 and tell both parties.

8. Platform service fee

8.1 Platform service fee: when funds are released, the task amount less the platform service fee enters the bank payout process. Rates, maximums, discounts, rate changes and fee documents for the platform service fee follow clause 6.3 of the Terms of Service (when this version was published, the rate was 12% of the task amount, up to A$45 per task, and on a task with a platform service fee discount, 2% of the task amount plus A$0.30). Each task is charged at the rate in force when the Service Provider submitted the offer or application for it, and a rate change does not affect tasks already formed or offers already submitted.

8.2 Payment processing costs: fees charged by the third-party payment provider for payments and for payouts to the Service Provider's bank account are included in the platform service fee. Whatever the payment provider actually charges, for example higher costs when the Task Poster uses an international card, the Service Provider's platform service fee is calculated only under clause 8.1 and the Platform bears any difference. The Platform does not charge it to the Service Provider separately, and does not calculate it separately by the type of card the Task Poster used.

8.3 Refunds and fees: when making a full refund, the Platform instructs the payment provider to refund the amount charged and reverse the corresponding transfer, and returns the full platform service fee for that payment. The Platform bears any payment processing fee that the payment provider does not return.

8.4 Amount shown to the Task Poster: the amount payable shown to the Task Poster before payment is the task amount agreed in the task contract, as a GST-inclusive total; any GST the Service Provider is required to charge is included. The Task Poster pays nothing else on top of it. The Service Provider can see the amount they will receive when making an offer or taking on a task, and can see the amounts received on their own earnings pages.

8.5 Chargebacks: a Task Poster may raise a chargeback with their card issuer. The card issuer handles it under the payment channel's rules, and the Platform submits the relevant records to the payment provider. A chargeback is not a Platform refund, and lawful chargeback rights are unaffected. The same amount is not reversed or refunded twice.

· Before release: the Platform may delay bank payout of the amount in question under clause 2.3 of the Terms of Service and reverse the amount covered by the chargeback through the payment provider. When it does so, it also returns the corresponding portion of the platform service fee, so the Service Provider does not bear the platform service fee for a chargeback before release. Once the chargeback has an outcome, the Platform settles in line with it; if the card issuer returns the funds, the Platform settles them in line with the release or refund decision already made and the payment channel's outcome.

· After release or bank payout: the Platform bears the chargeback. It does not recover released funds from the Service Provider unless the chargeback was caused by that Service Provider's fraud and recovery is permitted by law.

· Misuse: if the evidence establishes that chargebacks were misused to get around these Rules, the Platform may take action on the account under section 7.

8.6 Payment receipts and tax invoices: the Task Poster's payment receipt is issued by the Platform as the Service Provider's payment collection agent. It records the amount and status of the payment and is not a tax invoice. Whether GST applies to the task amount, and whether to issue a tax invoice, are the Service Provider's responsibility under the law. A Service Provider who is not registered for GST cannot issue a tax invoice.

9. Handling times (targets)

9.1 The following times are counted in business days and are the Platform's service targets, not guarantees:

· no-show reports and cases where a paid task cannot go ahead: first response within 1 business day; Platform handling within 2 business days;

· disputes about service quality: first response within 2 business days; Platform handling within 5 business days after complete evidence is received;

· refund requests: first response within 2 business days; a decision, or a request for further evidence, within 5 business days.

The longest time funds can stay unreleased is set by clause 4.5.

9.2 This section does not apply to emergencies such as personal safety. Contact local emergency services (000) immediately.

10. Safety and abuse reports

10.1 You may report safety concerns, misuse of address information or harassment in the app. The Platform treats these as a priority and keeps a record. In an emergency, see clause 9.2.

11. Relationship to other documents

11.1 These Rules form part of the Terms of Service and apply together with the Privacy Policy. On matters of payment, release and bank payout, refunds, disputes and account governance, these Rules prevail over the Terms of Service where they differ. On matters of personal information, the Privacy Policy prevails.

11.2 Version that applies: these Rules are recorded with each task contract, and the version recorded when the task contract is formed applies to that task (see clause 5.2 of the Terms of Service).
