Operator: BangBang Life Pty Ltd (ACN 703 821 738), referred to below as "the Platform", "we" or "us".

Where it applies: Australia.

Language: this Policy is issued in Chinese and in English. Both versions have equal effect. If the two versions differ in meaning, the English version prevails.

This Policy explains how we collect, use, disclose, store and protect your personal information through the BangBang app (万能帮帮 in Chinese), website and mini-program. Whether or not the Privacy Act applies to us as a matter of law, we handle personal information in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs), and we do not reduce the protection we give you because of our size.

In this Policy, a "Task Poster" is a user who posts a task, a "Service Provider" is a user who takes on a task, and a "Partner" is an independent promoter who works with the Platform. Other terms have the same meaning as in the Terms of Service.

1. Personal information we collect

We collect most personal information directly from you: what you give us when you register, post and take on tasks, chat, and set up payouts. We also obtain related information from our payment provider Stripe (payment status, card fingerprint identifiers, payout account IDs), from other users (reviews, reports, referrals) and from the public ABN register. Device and network information is generated automatically by the app.

· Identity and contact: name or nickname, mobile number (Australian +61), email address (optional link; once verified, used for account recovery and notices), account profile, and your public BB ID.

· Referral and attribution information: the BB ID of the user who referred you (any registered user may be entered as a referrer) or the referral code of a Partner, if you entered one when registering, and the referral or attribution recorded as a result. A referral by BB ID is used only to calculate referral rewards and to assess a referrer's application to become a Partner (see clauses 6.8 to 6.10 of the Terms of Service). Also: the single-use invitation codes you generate or use (we store an irreversible hash and the status, and also keep the last 3 characters of each code so that support staff can check which code is meant), the Regulars link records made through them, and records of BB Vouchers issued and used, records of where the Regulars benefit applied, and records of referral rewards.

· Tax reporting information (Service Providers): when you set up payouts, you give us in the app your legal name in English as shown on your identity document, your date of birth, residential address, email address and mobile number. If you are paid as a company (Pty Ltd), you give instead the company's ABN, registered name, trading name (optional) and address; the company's contact phone number and email address are the verified mobile number and email address on your account (if your account has no verified email address, you enter one in the form). ABN: optional if you are paid as an individual, required if you are paid as a company. If you give an ABN, we check that it is in a valid format, and Platform staff may also look it up on the public ABN register (ABN Lookup) to confirm that the registration matches the person or company you entered. We also record the ID of your payout account with Stripe and, for each quarter, the income you earn through the Platform, the number of tasks you complete and the platform service fee we charge you. We do not collect your tax file number (TFN). Identity verification and identity documents are handled by Stripe and we do not keep identity documents; Stripe handles that information under its own privacy policy (https://stripe.com/au/privacy).

· Partner details: if you apply to become a Partner, we collect the type of community group or account you give in your application, its member or follower count, and a link to it or a screenshot of it. We use this only to assess your application, and keep it for the period in clause 5.3. If you are a Partner, this also includes your ABN and GST registration status, your Partner share (the promotion reward the Platform pays a Partner out of the platform service fee it itself receives) and settlement records, and your payout details. Payout details are the payee name and PayID you give us (stored encrypted and used to pay your Partner share) or, if you are also a Service Provider paid through Stripe, the ID of your Stripe payout account. We do not collect a BSB or bank account number from Partners.

· Tasks and transactions: tasks posted or taken on, offers, contracts, records of payments and charges, releases, bank payouts and refunds, refund requests, no-show reports, disputes and evidence, reviews.

· Messages: your in-app chat with other users.

· Photos and files: the task photos and completion evidence photos you upload, and the images and files (PDF, text files and Office documents) you send in chat.

· Security and governance records: sign-in sessions and device information, phone-number change and account-recovery requests, email verification records, your block list, reports you make, cancellation and no-show records (such as cancellations after payment, no-shows and payment timeouts), warning, restriction, ban and appeal records, and the record left when the posting of a task, a chat message, a task description or an offer message is automatically blocked (the time, the task it concerned and the type of rule matched; we do not keep the blocked text; see clause 2.1).

· Location: addresses and coordinates connected with tasks, and the location of a Service Provider's device when the Service Provider submits completion of a task other than an online task (attending in person, pickup and delivery, or running an errand in a stated area) (see section 4).

· Device and usage: device and push identifiers, network (IP) addresses, logs and usage records. Search terms you type on the home page are used only for that search and are not saved. We do not use third-party analytics or advertising SDKs.

· Identifiers used for matching: to detect fake transactions and benefit abuse, we keep several kinds of one-way hash that can be compared but cannot be reversed: of a device (from a random device number the app generates on first launch), of a bank card (from the card fingerprint Stripe gives us; for Apple Pay or Google Pay, the wallet card on that device), of a Service Provider's payout bank account (from the account identifier Stripe gives us), of a Partner's PayID, and a supplementary identifier derived from the card brand and the last four digits of the card number. None of them can be turned back into a device number, card number, bank account number or PayID. See clause 2.1 for what they are used for and clauses 5.3 and 5.4 for how long we keep them.

· Errors and diagnostics: error and crash information from the app and servers (with personal information removed before sending; see section 3).

· Payment information: processed by a third-party payment provider. We do not store full card numbers or other sensitive payment credentials. We record only the payment amount and status and the settlement details we need, such as the card brand and the last four digits of the card number, the card's issuing country and card type, the result of 3-D Secure authentication, and the payment method (card, Apple Pay or Google Pay).

1.1 Information collected for sharing economy reporting

We collect the tax reporting information above because Subdivision 396-B of Schedule 1 to the Taxation Administration Act 1953 the Sharing Economy Reporting Regime (SERR), requires us to collect and report it. The identity and payout information collected when you set up payouts is reported to the Australian Taxation Office (ATO) under this regime twice a year, together with the income you earn through the Platform each quarter (see section 3). When we collect this information on the payout set-up page, we tell you this at the same time. When you set up payouts, we also give the relevant account-opening details to Stripe in advance, for opening your payout account and verifying your identity (see section 3). We keep this information for 5 years (in line with the general retention period for tax records), counted from the date we lodge the last report that contains it, and we keep it after your account is deleted (see clauses 5.3 and 5.4). If you do not provide it, you cannot set up payouts, and so cannot submit offers or take on tasks.

· Content checks and risk grading, fraud and abuse prevention, account security, and handling disputes and refunds.

· When handling complaints, disputes, refund requests and card chargebacks, Platform staff may view the chat messages connected with the matter. Each access must be linked to an existing report, dispute, refund request, support ticket or chargeback record and is logged.

· To respond to card chargebacks (where a cardholder disputes a payment through the card issuer), by preparing evidence about the transaction and giving it to Stripe.

· To meet legal, tax and compliance obligations, including reporting to the ATO under SERR.

· To apply the Regulars benefit, and to issue and apply BB Vouchers, to assess applications to become a Partner, to calculate and pay Partner shares, and to check that referrals and attributions are genuine.

· To find and fix faults and improve the Service.

· To communicate with you: verification codes, task and payment notices, security notices and support replies. We do not currently send promotional SMS or emails. If we decide to, we will first ask for your consent and give you a way to unsubscribe. The "Updates and recommendations" push notifications in the app are off by default, and you receive them only if you turn them on yourself.

2.1 Automated decisions

Some decisions are made by a computer program, or the program does the key step in them. These decisions can work in your favour or against you, and some affect your money, your account permissions or your ability to use the Service. This clause explains what personal information the programs use, which decisions are made entirely by a program, and which decisions are made by Platform staff after a program has done the key step.

(a) Personal information the programs use: records of the status and timing of tasks and payments; cancellation and no-show records; the text you submit (tasks, offer messages, chat, reviews, public nicknames, service introductions and tags, used only for checking); the hashed device, card and payout account identifiers (see "Identifiers used for matching" in section 1 of this Policy); and the hash of a mobile number (used for blocking only in the situations in clause 5.4 of this Policy).

(b) Decisions made entirely by a program

· Automatic confirmation and release: after the Service Provider submits completion, if the Task Poster has not confirmed by the automatic confirmation time, neither party has raised a dispute, and there is no unresolved dispute, chargeback, risk review or other matter needing a person to check, the system confirms automatically and releases the funds to the Service Provider (clause 3.2 of the Refund and Dispute Rules). The relevant times are notified in the app in advance.

· Handling at 85 days from payment: where the funds have not been released to the Service Provider 85 days after payment, the system automatically cancels the task and refunds the full amount to the Task Poster's original payment method. If a dispute or no-show report is still open at that point, the full amount is likewise returned to the Task Poster, and the Service Provider may still seek to recover it by lawful means. Where there is a card chargeback, Platform staff handle it (clause 4.5 of the Refund and Dispute Rules).

· Task Poster no-shows: if a Task Poster reported as a no-show does not respond within 24 hours, the Task Poster is treated as having admitted the no-show; the system automatically returns the payment and records one no-show (clauses 4.3 and 7.6 of the Refund and Dispute Rules).

· Warnings and restrictions based on the number of no-shows: according to the number of confirmed no-shows in the preceding 6 months, the system automatically records a written warning or a 30-day restriction. A Task Poster is restricted from posting or editing tasks, selecting offers and paying; a Service Provider is restricted from taking on tasks (clauses 7.6 and 7.8 of the Refund and Dispute Rules).

· Restriction while a case is open: while a dispute, refund request or no-show report on a task is open, the system automatically stops both parties from posting a new task or making a new offer or application, and lifts the restriction automatically when the case is closed; selecting offers and paying on their other tasks go on as usual (clause 6.8 of the Refund and Dispute Rules).

· Automatic rejection when the supplementary-materials deadline passes: a refund request or no-show report for which the requested materials have not been supplied by the deadline (48 hours) is automatically rejected or closed, and we tell you why (clause 6.9 of the Refund and Dispute Rules).

· Expiry of a selection: if there is no valid payment when the payment window closes after a Task Poster selects a Service Provider, the system makes the selection lapse and the task reopens (clause 5.3 of the Refund and Dispute Rules).

· First-task amount cap for new Task Posters: until a new Task Poster's first task has been completed and the funds released, the system does not allow a task, offer or selection above the cap (clause 5.1 of the Terms of Service).

· Content checks: when a task is posted, the system checks its content automatically. A task that does not pass, or that the system cannot confirm, is not published, and the system prompts you to edit and resubmit. A chat message, task description or offer message that contains a phone number, email address, WeChat or similar contact details, or wording about paying outside the app, is not sent; the system prompts you to edit it and leaves a record of the block (retention in clause 5.3 of this Policy). A review, public nickname, service introduction or tag that contains content that is not allowed is not accepted. Platform staff may refer to the record of a block when handling a related matter; the system does not restrict an account automatically because of it.

· Mobile number blocking: if, when an account was deleted, it was banned or under a no-show restriction, the hash of its mobile number is used during the stated period to stop the same number from registering again or being added to another account (see clause 5.4 of this Policy).

· Automated handling of platform service fee discounts and referral rewards: the system applies platform service fee discounts automatically to eligible tasks and issues referral rewards automatically when the conditions are met (clauses 6.8 and 6.9 of the Terms of Service). Partner shares are calculated automatically under the Partner Agreement.

(c) Decisions made by Platform staff after a program has done the key step

· Risk grading: when a task is posted, the system assigns a risk level. For a task marked for risk review, the payment is not released automatically and is passed to Platform staff for manual handling (clause 3.2 of the Refund and Dispute Rules).

· Matching on a shared card, device or payout account: before a referral reward is issued, the system checks whether the referrer, the referred user and the Task Poster of the relevant task share the same card (by card fingerprint hash; for wallet payments, the wallet card on that device), the same device or the same payout account. After a Regulars link is made, the system checks whether the newly registered account shares the same card, device or payout account with any other account on the Platform (including deleted accounts). Partner shares are checked in the same way. Where a match is found, the reward, discount or Partner share is held and passed to Platform staff for manual review; the system does not restrict an account or impose a penalty automatically because of a match.

· Third and later no-shows: Platform staff review whether to ban the account.

Account bans are always decided by Platform staff after a manual review of the evidence. The system never bans an account automatically.

Objecting: if you disagree with any of these decisions, you may contact us (see section 10 of this Policy) and ask Platform staff to review it manually. For a no-show record or a restriction, you may also appeal under clause 7.5 of the Refund and Dispute Rules. How to raise a dispute, refund request or no-show report is explained in sections 4 and 6 of the Refund and Dispute Rules.

3. How we disclose personal information

We disclose information only where necessary, to the following recipients:

· Other users (within a task): information shown to the other party as part of the task flow: public name and task content. After payment and confirmation, the necessary exact location is shown to the Service Provider. After payment is confirmed, the Task Poster and the matched Service Provider can see each other's mobile numbers so that they can contact each other directly.

· Other users (Service Provider profile): the Service Provider profile shown to signed-in users: public name, introduction, service tags, number of completed tasks, published reviews, a "New user" label, and "Repeat clients: N" (which counts only tasks completed through the Platform). Signed-in users can find the public profile of a Service Provider who has set up payouts, by BB ID, in the app and send an invitation; a Service Provider can generate and share their own name card, which contains only information from that public profile. "Repeat clients" means Task Posters who have, through the Platform, completed with that Service Provider two or more tasks, each of A$30 or more in a single task amount, released and not fully refunded, counted by person. The "New user" label means the user has fewer than 3 tasks completed in that role, paid out and with no open dispute; it is counted separately for a user's activity as a Service Provider and as a Task Poster. Beside a Service Provider's offer or application, the Task Poster also sees the Service Provider's cancellation count, which is the number of confirmed times the Service Provider cancelled after the Task Poster had paid plus the Service Provider no-shows the Platform has confirmed.

· Partners: a Partner receives only aggregate figures about the users they brought to the Platform (number of users, number of completed tasks and Partner share amount). They receive no user's name, mobile number, BB ID, task content or other personal information; where the number of users is very small, the number is shown only as a range. Your profile does not show whether you were brought to the Platform by a Partner.

· Payment provider (Stripe, United States): processing payments and charges, transferring the funds after deducting the platform service fee into the Service Provider's Stripe payout account, arranging bank payouts as instructed by the Platform, processing refunds and transfer reversals, and transferring Partner shares to Partners who are also Service Providers with a Stripe payout account. Bank cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay are accepted; the payment form does not enable Stripe Link or similar features that save card details and contact information. Stripe's app components collect device and network information under Stripe's own privacy policy, to prevent fraud.

· Stripe: payout set-up: when a Service Provider sets up payouts, we send Stripe the name, date of birth, address, email address and mobile number entered in the app (if the Service Provider is paid as a company: the company's registered name, ABN, address, phone number and email address, and the email address and mobile number of the company's representative), for opening the payout account and verifying identity, so you do not have to enter them again; you can confirm or change them on Stripe's pages.

· Stripe: card chargebacks: where a Task Poster raises a chargeback with their card issuer for a payment that has been released, we submit evidence in the Stripe dashboard: the contract, completion record and dispute record for that payment; the chat messages of both parties on that task; the Task Poster's account name, verified email address, mobile number (last three digits only), account creation date and the network address the payment was started from; and the card brand and last four digits. Stripe passes this evidence to the card issuer and card network. It does not include the Service Provider's mobile number, email address, bank details or ABN, or the task's full address.

· Our bank (Australia; used for PayID transfers): paying Partner shares to Partners who are paid by PayID (see "Partner details" in section 1). We give the bank only what the transfer needs: the payee name, the PayID and the amount.

· Wallet providers (Apple Pay: Apple, United States; Google Pay: Google, United States): when you choose to pay with Apple Pay or Google Pay, Apple or Google processes the payment token and passes it to Stripe to complete the payment.

· Maps provider (Google Maps Platform, United States): address standardisation, maps and service-area checks. The text you type in the address box is passed by our servers to Google for address suggestions and to look up coordinates, without your account details. The map is requested directly from Google by the Google Maps component on your device, and Google receives the information needed to show the map (for example, your device's network address and the map area).

· Push notification providers (Google Firebase Cloud Messaging, United States; Apple APNs, United States): app push notifications.

· SMS provider (Twilio, United States): verification codes, task notices and security notices.

· Email provider (Resend, United States): email verification codes (sent to the address you are linking), and notices and security emails (sent only to a verified linked email address).

· Error and performance diagnostics provider (Sentry, Germany): collecting error and crash information from the app and servers. Before sending, personal information such as mobile numbers, addresses, payment details and sign-in credentials is removed, and the provider's default collection of personal information is turned off.

· Operations monitoring and alerts provider (Better Stack, Germany): receiving the system alerts and operating heartbeats our servers send, and checking from outside that the service is online. Alerts and heartbeats contain only operational information such as task numbers, amounts, handling status and job counts, and do not contain mobile numbers, names, addresses or message content.

· App update provider (Expo EAS Update, United States): when the app starts, it checks with this provider for a newer version; it receives technical information such as the device's network address, app version and runtime version, but no account information.

We notify you before the handover is completed, and again at the handover, saying who the recipient is and that your information has been transferred. If you do not agree, you may delete your account under clause 6.3 before the handover (records the law requires us to keep are handled under clause 5.4).

4. Special handling of location information

4.1 The exact location of a task is revealed in stages. During browsing, offering, and after selection but before payment, no exact coordinates or address are provided; the public listing shows only an obscured approximate location (a fixed point within a grid cell: the cell is about 1 km across where many people live and is enlarged to several kilometres or even tens of kilometres where few people live, so that every cell holds at least about 50 dwellings) and a rough distance. How many dwellings an area has is worked out from the 2021 Census Mesh Block dwelling counts (the dataset Mesh Block Counts, 2021) published by the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), together with the matching Mesh Block boundaries. That data is provided under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence (CC BY 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) and has been aggregated by us; no user's personal information is involved. The aggregated result does not represent the views of the ABS, and the ABS does not endorse the Platform.

4.2 Only after payment and confirmation of the task is the necessary exact location shown to the matched Service Provider.

4.3 The full address and exact coordinates of a task are stored encrypted; public map markers are obscured; every access to an exact address is logged in an audit trail, and Platform staff view a full address only where customer service, safety or dispute handling needs it, with a record kept. The completion location (see clause 4.6) is stored separately, is viewed only by the Platform when handling a case, and every view is logged in an audit trail.

4.4 After a task is completed, the Service Provider can view the exact address only for 7 days after completion (for follow-up such as items left behind), and it is not shown to the Service Provider after that; the Task Poster can continue to see the address they entered.

4.5 If you choose "use my location" to see nearby tasks, your device's location is sent to our servers to filter tasks and work out distances; we do not add it to your account profile. Your location and search terms are used only for the query at hand and are not saved; our cloud infrastructure provider (section 3) may record the request in short-term technical logs.

4.6 Completion location: when a Service Provider submits completion of a task other than an online task (attending in person, pickup and delivery, or running an errand in a stated area), we read the location of the Service Provider's device once (latitude, longitude and accuracy) and keep it with the completion record, as evidence of completion and for review when handling disputes, refund requests and no-show reports. We read it only at the moment completion is submitted and do not track location continuously. A Service Provider who has not granted location permission cannot submit completion; where permission is granted but the location cannot be obtained at that moment, the Service Provider can still submit and we record "location not obtained". The completion location is not shown to the Task Poster or other users; only the Platform reviews it when handling those matters. We do not read a completion location for online tasks.

5. Storage, security and retention

5.1 Data is stored in Sydney, Australia (Amazon Web Services); see section 3.

5.2 Security measures include: encryption of communications between the app and our servers (HTTPS); encrypted storage of sensitive data such as addresses and Partners' PayIDs; access auditing; access controls; a second verification step for back-office operations; and sign-in session management.

5.3 Retention periods:

· Account profile: for the life of the account; after deletion, handled under clause 5.4.

· Your messages with other users (including the images and files in them), and reviews: 24 months from the end of the related task; if the task has a dispute, refund request or no-show report that is still open or has been closed for less than 24 months, the messages are kept with that case; after account deletion they are kept in de-identified form, with the sender shown as "Deleted user". Before a review is deleted at the end of its retention period, its star rating and count are added to the running totals, so the publicly displayed rating is not affected.

· Contracts, payment, settlement and refund records: 7 years from completion of the transaction (tax and accounting requirements).

· Tax reporting information and the reports we lodged: 5 years (in line with the general retention period for tax records), counted from the date we lodged the last report that contains the information.

· Regulars link records and the invitation codes that were used to make them: deleted once 30 days have passed since the Regulars benefit period ended (for a link that was revoked, once 30 days have passed since both the end of the period and the revocation; a link under Platform review is not deleted). After deletion, payment records still keep the platform service fee rate that applied and the reason it applied. An invitation code that was never used is kept until it expires or is withdrawn.

· Partner attribution records: once all three of the following are true, the identity of the attributed user is removed and we keep only a relationship record containing no personal information and a marker of whether the attributed person had completed a task. The three conditions are: the Partner share period has ended; there is no Partner share recorded under that attribution that is still unsettled; and 180 days have passed since the last Partner share entry (a release or an adjustment), which is the chargeback and recovery window (where there are no entries, this condition does not apply). Records that form the basis of a Partner share settlement are kept with the settlement records for 7 years.

· Personal referral records (referrals by BB ID): deleted once the referral reward has been issued or declined, the BB Vouchers issued under it have been used or voided, no review flag on it is open, and 12 months have passed since the referred person registered; before deletion, a referral that counts toward the number needed for a Partner application is added to the referrer's tally, a number that contains no personal information.

· Partner application information (type of group or account, member or follower count, link or screenshot): if the application is declined, 90 days from the decision; if it is approved, for as long as you are a Partner and for 24 months after your Partner status ends; if approved but the Partner Agreement is never accepted, 24 months from the approval.

· Partner payout details (payee name and PayID): until you change them, or your Partner status ends and all Partner shares have been paid; the record of each payment is kept with the settlement records for 7 years. The ABN and GST registration status you give us are kept with the settlement records for 7 years.

· Disputes, refund requests, no-show reports and their evidence (including the evidence documents we prepare to answer a dispute or a card chargeback): 24 months from the close of the case.

· Task photos: 24 months from the end of the related task; if the task has a dispute, refund request, no-show report or card chargeback that is still open or has been closed for less than 24 months, they are kept with that case.

· Completion photos and completion locations: 24 months from the end of the related task; where they become evidence in a dispute, refund request or no-show report, they are kept with that case's evidence.

· Report material: 90 days from the close of handling; where it led to account action, kept with the record of that action.

· Account actions (warnings, restrictions, bans) and appeal records: 24 months from the end of the action.

· Cancellation and no-show records (such as cancellations after payment, no-shows and payment timeouts): 24 months from when the record is made; where they led to account action, kept with the record of that action.

· Records of automatic blocks (the record left when the posting of a task, a chat message, a task description or an offer message is blocked): 24 months from the block.

· Audit logs and security records: 24 months. For the hashed identifiers used to recognise the same device, card or payout account, the 24 months are counted from the last time each was seen (for what happens after account deletion, see clause 5.4). Entries that record consents, Regulars links, Partner matters, payment, release, refund and dispute decisions are kept with the transaction records for 7 years, with the network (IP) address in them cleared after 24 months and the entries deleted after 7 years.

· Notifications in the app: 12 months from when sent.

· Channel send logs (records of SMS, email and push messages sent): 90 days from sending.

· Phone-number change and account-recovery request records: 24 months from when the request is closed.

· Sign-in sessions and device information: 12 months from last use.

· Support tickets and attachments: 24 months from when the ticket is closed.

· Privacy export files: 7 days from generation.

· The full address of a task: stored encrypted and kept for the same period as the contract records; the Service Provider can view it for 7 days after completion (see clause 4.4).

Information past its retention period is deleted or anonymised within a reasonable time; other records not listed above are likewise deleted or anonymised once they are no longer needed. Messages and reviews are deleted in their entirety when their retention period ends; when the retention period for a dispute, refund request, no-show report, or account action or appeal record ends, its text and evidence files are deleted and only a single result record containing no personal information is kept.

5.4 Retention after account deletion: after your account is deleted we still keep:

· the versions of the Terms and Privacy Policy you accepted, the account creation and deletion times, the account status, and the transaction and compliance records the law requires us to keep (see clause 5.3);

· the records you shared with the other party in a task (the task, messages, reviews and dispute records), kept in de-identified form for the periods in clause 5.3. De-identified means that we delete information that directly identifies you, such as your name, mobile number and email address, and keep the content of the record; the other party sees the sender or party shown as "Deleted user";

· tax reporting information: if you earned income through the Platform before deleting your account, we keep the tax reporting information described in section 1 to complete any report not yet lodged, for the period in clause 5.3; it is no longer linked to your public profile;

· referral and attribution relationships: only the relationship record, which contains no personal information, kept to complete any Partner share or benefit calculation not yet settled;

· hashes used for matching: one-way hashes of devices, bank cards and payout accounts (including a Partner's PayID), and the supplementary hash derived from the card brand and last four digits (see "Identifiers used for matching" in section 1). Purpose: only to prevent fake transactions and benefit abuse, that is, when we review referral rewards, Regulars benefits and Partner shares, to check whether a new account shares the same card, device or payout account with a deleted account (see clause 2.1 of this Policy). Period: 24 months from the last time the hash was seen, after which it is deleted. These hashes cannot be turned back into a card number or bank account number, and are no longer linked to your name, mobile number or email address;

· the hash of your mobile number: we keep a one-way hash of your mobile number only if, when your account was deleted, it was banned (a banned account can be deleted only after the appeal period has ended or the appeal has been decided and the ban is final; see clause 6.3 of this Policy) or was under a restriction for a Task Poster no-show or a Service Provider no-show (see clauses 7.6 and 7.8 of the Refund and Dispute Rules). The hash cannot be turned back into the number and is not linked to your account number. Its only use is to stop the same number being used to register again, or being added to another account, before that action ends: where the account was banned the hash is kept for 24 months, and where it was restricted the hash is kept until the restriction ends. When a number is stopped, you are asked to contact support. For any other account deletion, we do not keep a hash of your mobile number;

· backups: deleted data may remain in backups for up to 30 days.

5.5 Data breaches: if personal information is accessed, disclosed or lost without authority, we investigate immediately and take steps to contain the harm. Where we suspect an eligible data breach (one that is likely to result in serious harm to you and that remedial action cannot prevent), we assess it reasonably and promptly, and in any case within 30 days. If the assessment confirms it, we will, as soon as practicable, give a statement to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) and notify the people affected, telling them what happened, the likely consequences and the steps you can take; if we cannot notify each person, we will publish a notice on our website. We act in accordance with Part IIIC of the Privacy Act 1988 (the notifiable data breaches scheme).
